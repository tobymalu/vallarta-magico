Today, **November 3**, marks **International Day for Biosphere Reserves**, proclaimed by UNESCO to recognize places where nature conservation and community well-being coexist in balance. And we have a reason to celebrate close to home: since **2008**, the **Islas Marietas** archipelago has been part of UNESCO's World Network of Biosphere Reserves under the MaB Programme, right at the entrance to Banderas Bay.

## What does it mean to be a Biosphere Reserve?

The Biosphere Reserve designation is more than an honorary title: it's part of UNESCO's **Man and the Biosphere (MaB) Programme**, created to promote a balanced relationship between people and nature. Unlike a nature park closed off to the public, a Biosphere Reserve recognizes that real conservation only works when it includes the people who live off that land: fishermen, tour operators, and coastal communities.

In other words, protecting the Islas Marietas also means caring for the well-being of Banderas Bay as a whole, through scientific research, environmental education, and development that doesn't deplete the natural resources the region depends on.

## The natural richness of the Islas Marietas

What makes the Islas Marietas worthy of this distinction is the biodiversity they protect in such a small space. Its cliffs are home to the iconic **blue-footed booby**, a species that nests almost exclusively in areas free of land predators, like this one. Underwater, its coral reefs support a community of tropical fish and rays, and during whale season (December to March) the islands sit along the migration route of the humpback whales that come to the bay to breed.

## Conserving to thrive

Conserving the Islas Marietas isn't just a job for biologists and authorities: it's also a commitment for those of us who bring visitors to this archipelago every day. Responsible ecotourism — with limited quotas, CONANP-authorized wristbands, and trained crews — is a real conservation tool today: it generates income for local communities without relying on activities that harm the ecosystem.

That's why every well-run visit to the Marietas, with an operator that respects the park's rules, is in practice a way of supporting this UNESCO recognition so it continues to matter in the years ahead.

If you want to experience this natural heritage up close, do it through low-impact expeditions that respect the park's quotas and restricted zones. That's how we keep celebrating the Islas Marietas — not just on November 3, but every time someone steps into the water in this protected corner of Banderas Bay.
