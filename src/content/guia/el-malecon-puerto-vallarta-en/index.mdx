The Malecón isn't just a boardwalk facing the Pacific Ocean — it's the soul of Puerto Vallarta. Art, Mexican culture, gastronomy and nightlife all converge here. Lined by the sea on one side and traditional red-tile-roofed buildings on the other, walking along it is the first must-do activity for any visitor.

## What to do and see on the Malecón

- **The bronze sculptures.** The Malecón is an open-air museum. From the iconic Seahorse (the city's symbol) to The Boy on the Seahorse and The Millennia Rotunda, every piece has a story. Don't forget your camera.
- **The Papantla Flyers.** An ancient Mesoamerican ritual ceremony. Four men throw themselves off a pole, spinning around it tied by the feet while a fifth plays the flute from the top, 15 meters up. You'll find them near the start of the Malecón.
- **Shows at Los Arcos.** At the end of the traditional Malecón you'll find the famous stone arches facing Plaza de Armas. In the afternoons and on weekends, this open-air amphitheater fills with local musicians, clowns, dancers and painters.
- **Street food.** You can't leave without trying a "tuba" (a refreshing drink made from coconut palm sap and apple), esquites, or traditional cotton candy as you walk.

## The Malecón at night: life and parties

When the sun goes down, the Malecón transforms. It's the epicenter of Vallarta's nightlife. You'll find everything from international nightclubs with DJs and light shows to quiet bars where you can sip a margarita while listening to the waves hit the breakwater.

## Local tips for your visit

- **Best time to go:** go an hour before sunset. You'll avoid the midday heat, get perfect photo light, and enjoy Vallarta's famous sunset.
- **Accessibility:** the entire walk is flat and paved, making it perfect for wheelchairs, strollers and older visitors.
- **Connection:** at the south end of the Malecón, you'll cross the Cuale River bridge into the Zona Romántica (Olas Altas), another amazing area worth exploring.
